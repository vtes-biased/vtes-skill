## Question

We all want to have a nice game and have nobody play dirty tricks.

Just sometimes though, because time is a resource and managing it well can give you a win, we
find a situation where a player will surf the edge of the stalling line.

Stalling is of course a judgement call and a player doesnt need to play fast just because its
in someone elses best interest.

Its part of rulebook that a card is played by announcing its effects, and we have an internal
discussion on whether it can be accused of stalling to know just exactly which cards to remove
from ash heap when playing Shroud of Decay.

1) Is it stalling though?
2) Can a player get a warning for failing to announce their card effects repeatedly?
3) Is it a correct play to announce you remove the 7 cards at the top of the ash heap with
Shroud of Decay (probably the fastest way to announce a Shroud of Decay siperior)?

## Answer given

**(1) Choosing carefully is not stalling.** Which 7 cards leave the game is a real decision —
the removal is chosen by the player playing Shroud, and there is no order to the ash heap
[LSJ 20090710] — so denying Ashur Tablets fodder deserves deliberation. The judge lens: taking
*significantly more time than reasonably necessary* is **Slow Play** (Caution — the judge says
"please play faster", never "stalling"); **Stalling** proper is *intentional* clock
exploitation, whose key indicator is a behavior change while in an advantageous position or
near time, and it's a DQ. Careful selection at a consistent pace is neither.

**(2) Yes.** A card is played *by announcing its effects* (rulebook, Playing a Card). Repeated
failure to do so is a Communication Violation — incomplete information about public game
elements — penalized with a **Warning**; repeats don't escalate unless they significantly
impact gameplay, and intentional concealment for advantage crosses into Cheating.

**(3) Yes, it's a correct announcement** — with the understanding that since the ash heap has
no rules order, "the top 7" is merely a fast way of designating 7 specific physical cards,
which is exactly the choice the player is entitled to make. The cards are public and get
identified as they're removed. Conversely, a player who wants to pick precisely may take the
reasonable time that requires — speed is not owed just because someone benefits from it.
